— The House Position —

Data is the last unrepresented asset class.

The Data Company represents owners of proprietary data in exclusive licensing agreements with frontier AI laboratories. Appraisal, negotiation, closing — a broker's duty, a seller's interest.

Positions

I.

That frontier intelligence is trained on archives — and that the owners of those archives now hold a producing asset, whether or not they have priced it as one.

II.

That this asset is sold today without representation. The buyer fields a procurement desk, a legal team, and a price sheet. The seller fields an inbox.

III.

That between them sits the broker — whose duty runs to the seller alone: to appraise the corpus, to carry it to market under mandate, and to return with terms, not introductions.

A Letter to Data Owners

To the owners of archives —

Every laboratory building a frontier model wants what you hold. They will call it partnership, or access, or collaboration — and they will send a procurement desk whose entire profession is paying less for it. Nobody in that room is compensated to get you more. That is the seat we sit in.

We are not a marketplace, and we are not a list. We take a small number of archives to market under exclusive mandate, run the process ourselves, and are paid on the terms we win — never on making the introduction.

If your data has been approached before, appraise it before you answer. If it has not, it will be.

The Duties of the House

  1. Appraisal. To price the corpus before it is pitched — its rarity, its coverage, its provenance.
  2. Mandate. To carry one archive under one exclusive representation, so nothing leaks across the market.
  3. Negotiation. To compete qualified buyers against one another, and to refuse the first fair price for the best obtainable one.
  4. Revenue. To be paid on the outcome, and never on the introduction.
