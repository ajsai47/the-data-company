The Data Company · Imagery vocabulary

What the references say

Five references, decoded. Read together they describe a specific language: classical matter rendered in digital textures — engraving, halftone, pixel, wireframe — and a recurring subject: the moment of contact where value changes hands.

ref · concept
R.1

Money watching

Engraved currency · the tear

Franklin's eyes — the $100 bill's intaglio portrait — looking through torn paper. Engraving as presence: the currency is awake, evaluating. For a brokerage this is appraisal depicted — the instrument that sees what you're holding.

Lives at: the appraisal moment — "Find how much your data is worth" under the gaze of the instrument itself. Also a candidate for the letter or a banner break.
Note: stock image (watermarked); final would need licensing or a recreated tear effect over PD currency engraving.

R.2

The touch

Contact moment · human × machine

The Creation of Adam recast: human hand reaching to a robotic hand, a spark at the contact point, stipple-engraved on a starfield. Same subject as your pixelated version — but the "other side" is explicitly machine. The deal as first contact.

Lives at: hero candidate — arguably the most literal "data meets dollars" image. Stippled black ground suggests a dark hero variant.
Note: reference only; recreate via commission or composite — PD engraving of a hand + rendered machine hand.

R.3

The deal, annotated

Handshake · halftone → wireframe · UI chrome

A handshake where one party is halftone dots and the other is wireframe mesh — analog meeting digital — wrapped in technical annotation: "security*", "trust001", "approved", orbit circles, a single orange node. This is the most complete reference: the brokerage's entire business in one image, and the annotation layer is a design vocabulary you could own outright.

Lives at: the negotiation/mandate section — the moment the mandate is signed. The annotation chrome ("appraisal*", "mandate_01", "closed") could become a recurring device across all figures.
Note: reference only; the annotation vocabulary is yours to take, the image itself needs recreation.

R.4

Intaglio macro

Engraving texture as field

Extreme close-up of currency engraving — hatching dense enough to read as pure texture, almost like signal noise. Engraving at macro scale becomes abstract data: line-work indistinguishable from code.

Lives at: texture bands — section dividers, footer grounds, plate edges. Anywhere the page needs "material."
Note: easily recreated — PD currency engravings photographed/cropped at macro scale, or generated hatching.

R.5

Dithered portrait

Halftone · the analog→digital bridge

A portrait rendered entirely in halftone dots on black — the image held in a suspended state between photograph and data. The dither IS the fusion: nothing classical, nothing digital, just the threshold between them.

Lives at: portraits — of sellers, of corpus subjects, of "the archive" personified. A plate style for figurative moments.
Note: trivially reproducible — any photograph run through ordered-dither/halftone.

The system

Subject

The contact moment — hands touching, shaking, appraising. A brokerage's whole business is the instant value changes hands.

Texture

Halftone, stipple, intaglio hatching, pixel blocks — the dither as the bridge between classical matter and data.

Chrome

Technical annotation — "trust001", "security*", "approved" — orbit diagrams and nodes as a designed layer over the figures.

Ground

Four of five sit on black. The images are vaults — dark chambers inside a light institutional page.

The structure this implies: the page is paper; the pictures are vaults. Keep the charter document on white — serif, hairlines, positions. Let every image be a dark chamber: halftone, engraved, annotated. The contrast between light document and dark imagery is itself the fusion — institution on the surface, transaction underneath.
